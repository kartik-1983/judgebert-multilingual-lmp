# PreserveLex Dataset Card

## Dataset identity

- **Name:** PreserveLex English–Hindi Legal Meaning Preservation Dataset (working title)
- **Task:** Estimate whether a simplified legal clause preserves the material legal meaning of its source.
- **Languages intended:** English and Hindi.
- **Domains currently represented:** insurance and contract-style clauses.
- **Current status:** Exploratory, template-generated dataset; not a validated benchmark.

## Current audited snapshot

The current CSV snapshot contains 334 rows: 167 English and 167 Hindi. The audit found 12 unique original clauses in each language, 26 unique English simplifications, 27 unique Hindi simplifications, and 53 unique language/original/simplified combinations.

The generator randomly selects from a small set of templates, assigns a domain randomly, selects a good/bad quality category randomly, and samples a score from a predefined range. It also adds identical and unrelated sanity examples.

## Intended use

The current snapshot may be used for:
- testing data loaders and training scripts;
- testing grouped split code;
- checking output formats and visualizations;
- controlled sanity checks, clearly separated from real evaluation.

It must not be used to claim:
- human-validated legal meaning-preservation performance;
- generalization to unseen statutes, contracts, jurisdictions, or legal domains;
- superiority over JUDGEBERT or other metrics;
- reliable legal advice or automated legal compliance decisions.

## Labels

Current lmp_score, decision, quality, and explanations are generated by the project template-generation process. They are not independent human annotations. Scores are sampled from quality-dependent ranges, so a model may learn the generation rules rather than legal semantics.

Do not merge these fields into human reference labels. Preserve them under names that identify them as synthetic labels, or exclude them from human evaluation.

## Known limitations

1. Very small number of unique source clauses.
2. Repeated original and simplified sentences.
3. Random domain assignments do not establish domain diversity.
4. Generated score ranges encode the generator's assumptions.
5. Provenance for source clauses is not established by the current audit.
6. Cross-language semantic equivalence and translation alignment have not been independently verified.
7. Human reviewer agreement has not yet been measured.

## Required improvements

- Attach verifiable source metadata to each legal clause.
- Expand to diverse source documents and unique clauses.
- Have qualified/appropriately trained reviewers independently annotate the pairs.
- Record raw scores, rationales, error dimensions, confidence, and adjudication.
- Create document/clause-grouped splits, including cross-language grouping for parallel clauses.
- Maintain a separate held-out, human-annotated test set.
- Publish evaluation results with language-specific metrics and limitations.

## Ethical and legal considerations

Legal text can be jurisdiction-specific and context-dependent. Use only material with a clear lawful basis for collection and redistribution. Redact personal or confidential information. Do not treat model predictions as legal advice. Reviewers should be told that scores are research annotations and not legal determinations.

## Versioning

Every release should include a version, date, source manifest, annotation guideline version, split manifest, and changelog. Never overwrite raw annotations or silently replace labels.
